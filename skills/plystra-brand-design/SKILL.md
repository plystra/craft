# Plystra brand design

Use Craft to make identity decisions appropriate to the work's medium, audience, and existing character.

## Establish the applicable scope

Use the project's maintained relationship record and the [scope definitions](references/README.md#scope-and-project-relationships). Owned projects and sub-brands follow the full applicable standard; sponsored projects retain their chosen adoption scope and separate affiliation obligations. Voluntary adoption grants no affiliation or brand permission.

Read the [requirement levels and applicability](references/ADOPTION.md) when that context is not already established. This entry point routes work; it does not create requirements, replace platform instructions, or expand the user's authorized task. A focused identity review is not proof of full Craft compliance.

## Choose the relevant references

- For positioning and the relationship between parent and project identities, read [Brand Philosophy](references/principles/01-brand-philosophy.md).
- For marks, typography, palette, imagery, or motion, read [Visual Identity](references/principles/04-visual-identity.md).
- For names, taglines, or public descriptions, read [Language and Writing](references/principles/03-language-and-writing.md).
- For digital interaction or visual accessibility, read [Accessibility and Interaction](references/principles/06-accessibility-and-interaction.md).
- For sponsorship wording, permissions, licensing, or official status, read [Governance and Legal](references/principles/11-governance-and-legal.md).
- Use the [Charter](references/CHARTER.md) when reconsidering the work's purpose or admission under Plystra.

Load the branches the task needs. The [reference index](references/index.md) lists every bundled source and when it applies.

## Work from the actual identity

1. Inspect the accepted identity assets, existing public surfaces, project purpose, and constraints before proposing changes. Distinguish an accepted design from an unselected candidate.
2. Identify the decision being made: positioning, affiliation, visual system, or a particular asset. Preserve the requested boundary between refining an identity and replacing it.
3. Apply the selected principles to that decision. A sub-brand may have its own personality; use evidence about audience and purpose instead of making every project imitate the parent palette or mood.
4. Produce the requested assets or review. Explain consequential choices through legibility, recognition, appropriate expression, accessibility, and honest representation.
5. Inspect the result in its intended medium and representative sizes. Check affiliation wording against the confirmed relationship and asset licensing against the actual provenance.

Use the [decision-record template](assets/templates/decision-record.md) when a durable identity decision needs a record under the project's workflow. Do not create process documents merely because a template is bundled.

## Deliver with traceable scope

State what changed or was reviewed, the accepted constraints, verification performed, and remaining decisions. Distinguish candidates from approved identity assets and an authorized sponsorship acknowledgement from ownership.
