# Plystra project website

Deliver a public website that explains the project accurately and works for people, search engines, and systems that share its content.

## Scope

Apply this workflow to Plystra work or an explicitly adopted Craft scope: owned projects, sub-brands, and every project under them follow all requirements applicable to their actual media, surfaces, and activities; sponsored projects choose their adoption scope while separate sponsorship admission and affiliation rules still apply. Preserve requirement levels: `must`, `required`, unqualified `never`, and unqualified procedural instructions are requirements; `should`, `recommended`, `prefer`, `avoid`, and `suggested` are recommendations, with reasons recorded for material alternatives; `may`, `optional`, and `can` expressing a choice are options. Lists inherit their introducing statement, while an explicit `must` still creates a requirement. `Should never` is a recommendation; examples and suggested scales add no independent requirements, and recommendations cannot weaken requirements. This focused workflow neither grants affiliation nor establishes full Craft compliance. Stay within the request and existing authorization, respect trusted project guidance and platform instructions, and do not treat this skill as permission to publish or make unrelated changes.

## Establish the public surface

Identify the official origin, real public pages, existing identity, intended languages, project status, publisher, and confirmed ownership or sponsorship. Distinguish indexable content from private, authenticated, preview, and intentionally excluded pages. An official website may represent any medium of work; these rules do not require every project to create a website.

Read [website delivery](references/website-delivery.md) for the complete public-site requirements and acceptance checks. Use [public-site copy](references/public-site-copy.md) for descriptions and metadata, [accessible pages](references/accessible-pages.md) for usable interaction, and [site identity assets](references/site-identity-assets.md) for actual logos and truthful screenshots. Check [website claims and notices](references/website-claims-and-notices.md) for publisher, affiliation, privacy, and service wording. If forms, analytics, support, or other features handle real user data, use [collection and processors](references/collection-and-processors.md) before the relevant collection or disclosure begins.

## Implement and inspect the site

Keep visible text, metadata, canonical links, sitemap entries, structured data, and summaries consistent with current capabilities. Indexable pages need primary content and metadata in their initial HTML. Every official site must publish deliberate `robots.txt`, a valid `sitemap.xml`, and public `/llms.txt`; the summary complements the other surfaces and never substitutes for them. Provide accurate sharing metadata, accessible preview images, recognizable site identity, and truthful JSON-LD.

Use the simplest rendering approach that meets the site and its maintenance needs. Keep intended public origins consistent, use proper redirects and error statuses, and treat indexing controls as distinct from access controls. Work within the site's accepted visual identity; replacing the brand or designing an unrelated application interface is a separate task.

Verify the production output and actual responses available in the authorized environment: HTML content and metadata, status codes, robots/sitemap/llms consistency, sharing assets, JSON-LD, and representative accessible pages. Inspect desktop and mobile behavior and reduced motion as relevant. Run deployed checks after an authorized publish, or on an existing deployment in scope; a successful local build is not evidence that the new site is live.

Deliver the site changes or concrete findings with the routes and checks covered. Separate local evidence, deployed evidence, and remaining checks. Do not publish or change domains unless the request or established authorization covers it.
