# Plystra Charter

This charter states why Plystra exists and what its work commits to. Who it binds is defined in [scope and project relationships](README.md#scope-and-project-relationships).

## 1. Identity

Plystra is an independent practice for thoughtful, durable work across software, objects, tools, writing, research, and experiments.

It exists to make things people can use, understand, care for, and keep. The medium may change; responsibility for the work and its effect on people remains.

Plystra favors tools that are small enough to reason about, strong enough to rely on, and calm enough to live with.

## 2. Long-term ambition

Plystra should grow toward the seriousness of a foundation, the taste of a design-led studio, and the reliability of a mature institution that preserves and shares knowledge.

It should not imitate the surface of a large company before earning the substance of one. The brand should feel precise, restrained, and permanent even when the organization is still small.

## 3. Core belief

Good work should remain understandable beyond the moment it is made.

A work's maturity comes from whether its purpose, construction, limits, and care can be explained. Software, physical objects, and published ideas need different forms of maintenance and preservation, but each deserves that consideration.

## 4. What Plystra builds

Plystra projects may include:

- software and digital systems;
- physical objects and tools;
- writing, publications, and documentation;
- research and studies;
- experiments that clarify a question, material, method, or interaction;
- work combining several of these forms.

No medium is required to imitate another; [applicability follows the work](ADOPTION.md#3-applicability-follows-the-work).

A Plystra project does not need to be large. It must be cared for.

## 5. What Plystra avoids

Plystra avoids:

- trend-driven work with no plan for care or preservation;
- interfaces that trade clarity for spectacle;
- copywriting that exaggerates impact;
- lock-in disguised as simplicity;
- unnecessary platform behavior;
- systems that hide irreversible actions;
- AI features that cannot explain what they did;
- public roadmaps written mainly for hype;
- engineering decisions that make future maintenance harder without a clear reason.

## 6. The standard of care

Every project under Plystra should be treated as if it may still matter in five years.

That means:

- names should be chosen carefully;
- construction, methods, interfaces, and APIs should be documented where relevant;
- migrations should be reversible when reasonable;
- data ownership should be respected;
- safety and security concerns should have an obvious reporting path;
- releases should communicate risk;
- interfaces should not surprise users for the sake of novelty;
- contributors should be able to understand the system without private context.

## 7. Public posture

Plystra should speak with confidence, not volume.

The public voice should be calm, technical when useful, plain when possible, and never desperate for attention.

Plystra may be ambitious, but it should not be grandiose. It should let the quality of its work create authority.

## 8. Project admission

A Plystra-owned project or a project under a Plystra sub-brand must meet all of these conditions:

1. It expresses a long-term idea, not merely a one-off implementation.
2. It can be maintained with available resources.
3. It benefits from being understood as part of the Plystra family.
4. It can be explained without relying on hype.
5. It has a plausible path to documentation, support, and responsible stewardship.
6. It commits to the full philosophy, with clear responsibility for meeting its requirements.

A project should not carry the Plystra name only because it was created by the same person. It should carry the name because it shares the same care, restraint, and long-term direction.

A Plystra sub-brand must also document its identity, responsible steward, and relationship to Plystra.

Sponsored projects are admitted under the separate [sponsorship requirements](principles/11-governance-and-legal.md#7-sponsorship-admission-and-continuation).

## 9. Project retirement

Retiring a project is acceptable. Abandoning a project silently is not. A retired project follows the [retirement requirements](principles/10-release-and-maintenance.md#9-retirement).

## 10. The Plystra promise

Plystra does not promise to build everything.

It promises to build with care, explain decisions, respect users, and avoid confusing noise for progress.
